Lab · applied experiments

The methods still work. The ground moved.

Suppose, for a moment, the whole floor is standardized — legible, documented, measured. What then? Do you just run SMED, Kaizen and 5S the way they were always run?

The methods aren't the problem; they're sound. But they were built for a complicated world — knowable cause and effect, a stopwatch and a board enough to see what's happening. The floor now generates more data than anyone can read, and observation itself is becoming continuous and almost free. That's a complex environment now, not a complicated one — and running an unchanged method on it is the right tool in the wrong domain.

So the Lab isn't "AI replaces the method." The method stays. AI changes the domain it operates in: it eats the complicated half — the seeing, the timing — and leaves the complex half exposed, the half that always mattered and no one had time for. Each video is a vision of how that could look.

Question

What does SMED become when every changeover is observed, continuously, with no stopwatch?

Method in the new environment

A camera classifies every action as internal or external across hundreds of runs, surfacing the pattern no sample could. The engineer stops timing and starts deciding.

Industrial context

A high-mix line, a changeover every few hours. The traditional study catches a handful; the variation lives in the hundreds that run unobserved.

What stays human

The camera sees that a wait repeats; only the operator knows why. Judgment and trust to change it stay human.

Limits

Not a working vision system; figures are illustrative, not measured from the footage. It shows where the analysis would point.

What it shows

The method survives intact; observation becomes total and free, and attention moves to the complex "why".

Question

What happens when the standard can see whether it's being followed — and tell you why it isn't?

Method in the new environment

The system compares the observed sequence to the documented standard and surfaces the drift and its pattern — not to police the worker, but as data about the standard.

Industrial context

A station with the method on a screen (Case 01). It's visible, yet the real method still drifts — a step skipped, a quiet workaround.

What stays human

Deciding if a drift is a fault to fix or an improvement to adopt — and the trust that makes an operator show you the workaround, not hide it.

Limits

Illustrative, not real adherence detection. The point is the reframe: drift as signal, not sin.

What it shows

Visual management's next step: the standard that reads itself. The human curates it instead of enforcing it.

Question

What does an analysis see when it reads all the metrics together — the way no single dashboard, or person, ever does?

Method in the new environment

It reads the metrics jointly and flags the contradiction a human would need months and a hunch to find — the rework hiding between OEE and cost.

Industrial context

A control room: OEE green, energy flat, cost creeping. Three owners, three views, a loss living in the gap between them.

What stays human

Confronting what the incoherence means — and the culture that wanted it hidden. The machine finds it; the person navigates the fix.

Limits

Illustrative, not a real anomaly engine. It shows the kind of question worth asking.

What it shows

The highest-value move: making the relationships between metrics legible. Human work becomes interpreting, not calculating.

What the Lab is really about

Not that AI is coming for the floor — but where human attention goes once it arrives. The methods endure; the complex, human half is finally the part we have time to think about.
